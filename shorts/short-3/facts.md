Story type: real

In 1924, a 28-year-old Portuguese man named Artur Virgilio Alves dos Reis pulled off the largest counterfeiting fraud in European history, creating money that was completely genuine.
Source: Bank of Portugal history, PMG Notes, Wikipedia

Instead of printing fake notes on cheap presses, Reis forged a top-secret government contract and went directly to Waterlow and Sons in London, the official company that printed banknotes for the Bank of Portugal.
Source: Thomas Numismatics, Wikipedia

Reis traced the signatures of Bank of Portugal governors from actual currency, had the forged contract notarized and stamped by embassies, and convinced the printers that the banknotes were for a secret government investment project in Angola.
Source: Algarve History Association, PMG Notes

Waterlow and Sons used the official Bank of Portugal printing plates and watermarked paper to print two hundred thousand banknotes of five hundred escudos each, totaling one hundred million escudos—roughly one percent of Portugal's entire economy.
Source: PMG Notes, Wikipedia

Reis collected trunks filled with genuine currency, opened his own bank named Banco de Angola e Metropole, bought estates, businesses, and jewelry, and began buying shares in the Bank of Portugal itself to control the central bank and conceal the duplicate serial numbers.
Source: PMG Notes, Wikipedia

In late 1925, an investigation discovered identical serial numbers on banknotes in circulation. Reis was arrested and sentenced to twenty years. The Bank of Portugal sued Waterlow and Sons in London in a landmark trial and won massive damages.
Source: Bank of Portugal v. Waterlow & Sons Ltd [1932] AC 452, Wikipedia
