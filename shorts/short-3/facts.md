# Facts: The Ship That Grew A Weird Nose (Bulbous Bow)

- **The Real Principle:** Adding a bulbous projection on the bow of a ship below the waterline creates a secondary wave. When designed properly for the ship's operating speed, this secondary wave destructively interferes with the natural primary bow wave created by the hull, flattening the water in front of the vessel and reducing wave-making drag by 12% to 15%.
- **Historical Origin:** Developed by American naval architect David W. Taylor in the early 20th century (first tested on the battleship USS Delaware in 1910). Contemporary designers ridiculed the strange underwater bump as grotesque and counterintuitive, believing only sharp, knife-edge bows could cut through water cleanly.
- **Modern Use:** Virtually every modern cargo freighter, supertanker, cruise ship, and naval combatant features a bulbous bow today.
