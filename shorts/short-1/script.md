In two thousand and six, a man mails himself out of a federal prison. Not in an envelope. In a shipping pallet full of mailbags.

His name doesn't matter. What matters is this is his third escape. The first time, he greases his hands with lip balm, slips out of handcuffs, and jumps from a third-story window. The second time, he crawls through a ventilation duct. Both times they catch him. Both times they put him somewhere tighter.

So now he's in a federal penitentiary in Louisiana, serving two life sentences, and his job is fixing torn mailbags in the prison shop. Every day he watches pallets of repaired bags get shrink-wrapped and shipped out the front gate on a forklift. Every single day.

You see, he doesn't dig a tunnel. He doesn't climb a wall. He builds a hidden compartment inside one of those pallets, threads a breathing tube to the outside, and climbs in. The guards shrink-wrap the whole thing themselves. They load it onto the forklift themselves. They wave it through the gate themselves.

When the warehouse crew leaves for lunch, he cuts through the plastic, steps out, and jogs away in running gear.

Here's the part nobody believes. A cop spots him jogging along a railroad track that same day. Pulls over. Rolls the dashcam. And this guy, the most wanted fugitive in the country, looks the officer dead in the eye and says, "Name's Jimmy. I'm just out for a run." The cop lets him go.

He stays free for over a year before the Mounties catch him in Canada. They don't let him fix mailbags anymore.
