In nineteen ninety-six, a college student tries to buy a thirty-million-dollar military fighter jet with soda points. And legally, he almost pulled it off.

It starts with a national television commercial. Pepsi launches a rewards program where you collect points from bottle caps to win hats, jackets, and sunglasses. But at the end of the ad, a teenager lands a military Harrier jump jet right outside his high school. Across the bottom of the screen, the text reads: Harrier Fighter, seven million Pepsi points.

To drink seven million sodas, you would need to drink roughly one hundred and ninety cans every single day for one hundred years. Nobody can do that.

You see, a twenty-one-year-old business student named John flips to the back of the catalog and spots a tiny loophole in the fine print. You can buy extra points directly from Pepsi for ten cents each, as long as you mail in fifteen real points.

He does the math. Seven million points at ten cents each is seven hundred thousand dollars. A brand-new jump jet costs over thirty million.

So he convinces five wealthy investors to back him, buys fifteen cans of soda, and mails Pepsi a certified check for seven hundred thousand and eight dollars. He demands his fighter jet.

Pepsi freaks out. They reject his check and claim the ad was an obvious joke. So John sues them in federal court for breach of contract.

The judge eventually ruled that no reasonable person would believe soda cans could buy a weapon of war. But just to be safe, Pepsi immediately updated the commercial to seven hundred million points.
