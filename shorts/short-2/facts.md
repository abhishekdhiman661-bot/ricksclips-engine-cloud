Story type: real

In 1996, Pepsi launched the "Pepsi Stuff" promotional campaign, where buyers collected points to redeem for t-shirts, leather jackets, and sunglasses.
Source: Leonard v. Pepsico, Inc., 88 F. Supp. 2d 116 (S.D.N.Y. 1999), Wikipedia

The TV commercial featured a teenager flying a military AV-8B Harrier II jump jet to school with the caption: "HARRIER FIGHTER: 7,000,000 PEPSI POINTS". The jet was valued at roughly thirty million dollars.
Source: CBS News, Wikipedia

The official rules allowed participants to purchase additional points for ten cents each, as long as they submitted at least fifteen original points.
Source: Leonard v. Pepsico, Inc.

21-year-old business student John Leonard noticed the loophole: seven million points at ten cents each equaled seven hundred thousand dollars.
Source: CBS News, Quimbee

Leonard raised seven hundred thousand dollars from five investors, filled out the order form with fifteen Pepsi points, and mailed a certified check for seven hundred thousand and eight dollars and fifty cents.
Source: Leonard v. Pepsico, Inc., CBS News

Pepsi rejected the order as a joke and sent back coupons. Leonard sued Pepsi for breach of contract, demanding delivery of the fighter jet.
Source: Justia, CBS News

In 1999, Federal Judge Kimba Wood ruled in favor of Pepsi, stating that no reasonable person would conclude the commercial was a serious contractual offer. Pepsi subsequently changed the commercial's price tag to 700,000,000 points.
Source: Leonard v. Pepsico, Inc., Wikipedia
